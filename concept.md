# UNPLUG TO RECONNECT

### One week offline. One week of real life.

**Unplug to Reconnect** is a one-week outdoor experience for **young adults aged 18–25**, set in a **small rural setting in the Carpathian Mountains of Romania**.

At 18–25, young people are making some of their first major decisions:

*What should I study? What kind of work do I want? Where do I want to live? What matters to me? What kind of person do I want to become?*

At the same time, much of what they see, hear and compare themselves with comes through a screen.

**We don't offer answers. We create an environment in which you might find your own direction, inspiration or perspective through real, face-to-face interaction.**

For one week, participants step away from the digital noise and return to something fundamentally human: **doing, experiencing, talking face-to-face, spending time in nature and sharing real life with other people.**

---

## What do you take home?

The week is built around three outcomes:

### 📵 Digital reset

Smartphones and social media take a back seat. The idea isn't to reject technology, but to experience what happens when it stops occupying the center of everyday life.

### 💪 Confidence

Participants hike, cook, camp, learn practical skills, take responsibility, deal with manageable discomfort and discover what they can do for themselves.

### 🤝 Real human connection

A small group of **10–12 participants and two hosts** lives together for a week. They talk, work, eat, disagree, laugh, play, hike and solve problems together—in person.

---

## Learn by doing — and by discussing

Each morning starts slowly with breakfast, followed by a **workshop**.

The workshops take different forms. Some are **hands-on and practical**, focused on learning a skill by actually doing it. Others are **discussion and debate workshops**, creating space to explore ideas, exchange perspectives and challenge assumptions.

### Some ideas we have for practical workshops:

**Building and managing a campfire · Planning and preparing for a mountain hike · Navigation in the wild · Pitching and organizing a camp · Understanding healthy food and nutrition · Basic woodworking and craftsmanship · Learning traditional skills and ways of working the land**

These workshops could be led by a **mentor with practical experience or expertise**—someone who can demonstrate a skill, work alongside the participants and help them learn by doing.

### Some ideas we have for discussion and debate workshops:

**Relationships · Gender · Feminism · Masculinity · Climate change · Politics · Food and consumption · Capitalism · Society · Responsibility · The influence of social media · What success means · The choices we make as we enter adulthood**

These sessions don't necessarily need an expert with answers. Some might have a **mentor with relevant knowledge or experience**, while others might simply have a **moderator** whose role is to introduce the subject, create a respectful environment, ask good questions and make sure different perspectives have space to be heard.

**Our role is to create the environment, not dictate the content.**

The aim is to give participants space to **think, participate, question, disagree, listen and form their own views**—face-to-face, without a social-media feed or algorithm shaping the conversation.

The surrounding rural community can become part of the learning experience as well. Local people and craftspeople could share knowledge and stories about **food, agriculture, woodworking, traditional crafts, music, culture and skills accumulated over generations**.

These are **some of the ideas we have**, not a fixed curriculum. The workshops can evolve depending on the participants, the mentors available and the place in which each camp takes place.

---

## And there is plenty of time to do nothing

After preparing and eating lunch together, afternoons deliberately slow down.

Read. Sleep. Play cards or board games. Walk. Talk. Help in the garden. Sit somewhere. Have a ridiculous conversation. Have an important one.

There doesn't need to be an activity every hour.

**Learning how to spend unstructured time without reaching for a smartphone is part of the experience.**

Everyone also contributes to running the camp: preparing meals, cleaning, organizing equipment and looking after the place.

Dinner is prepared together. Evenings belong to campfires, games, conversations, music and stars.

---

## From village to wilderness

The setting is an important part of the concept.

The base is in a **real rural community in the Romanian Carpathians**—not an isolated resort built for tourists.

There is an old house or simple base, a garden, tents and the everyday life of a mountain village.

And then there are the mountains.

One of the defining experiences of the week is a **one-night mountain expedition starting directly from camp**.

No excursion bus. No driving to a tourist attraction.

Participants shoulder proper hiking backpacks and **leave on foot**. They carry their tents, food, equipment and sleeping bags into the mountains, establish camp somewhere spectacular, spend the evening and night outside, watch the stars and sunrise, pack everything again and walk home.

The camp provides the necessary equipment, including **proper backpacks, reliable tents and sleeping bags suitable for temperatures around 0°C**.

There is challenge and intentional discomfort—but this isn't survival training. Experienced leadership, appropriate equipment, planning and safety remain fundamental.

---

## Phones down. Cameras up.

We invite participants to leave their smartphones behind for most of the experience. The phones are stored safely and remain available when needed.

There are designated opportunities to contact family and friends, and the camp remains reachable in an emergency. The intention is not to demonize technology, but to intentionally create a little distance from it—just for a few days.

Memories still matter. One idea we have is to give participants **digital cameras** instead of using their phones for photographs. At the end of the experience, they take the **memory card** home with them.

**Capture the experience without experiencing it through a smartphone.**

---

## International by design

The camp's common language is **English**.

Participants can come from Germany, Romania and elsewhere.

For non-native speakers, English becomes something they use naturally—while hiking, cooking, arguing a point in a workshop or sitting around a fire—not something they study for a test.

Someone your age from another country may be wrestling with many of the same questions and challenges—and may see them completely differently.

**That diversity of background and perspective is part of the value of the experience.**

---

## Start small. Keep it real.

### The pilot

The first version is deliberately intimate:

**10–12 people · 18–25 years old · 1 week · Romanian Carpathians · English-speaking**

The point is not to build a big program from day one.

We start small, stay agile and treat the first camp as a pilot: something real enough to test the concept, but small enough to learn and adapt quickly.

The aim is to learn what works, what doesn't, what participants value most and what should evolve before the concept grows.

A week to meet people.  
A week to use your hands.  
A week to challenge yourself.  
A week to walk into the mountains.  
A week to have conversations without an algorithm deciding what comes next.

---

## Our vision

We want **Unplug to Reconnect** to become more than a one-week camp.

The long-term vision is to create a **permanent rural home in the Romanian Carpathians**—a place where people can regularly step away from digital life, reconnect with nature, learn practical skills and spend meaningful time with others.

A place with an old house, a barn, tents, a garden and an orchard.

A place that is simple, rooted in its surroundings and connected to the local community.

Over time, several camps could take place throughout the year, each following the same core philosophy while allowing the seasons to shape the experience.

Participants might come in spring to prepare the garden and plant.

Others might return in summer to work, hike and watch things grow.

Later in the season, they might harvest, cook and eat food that was grown on the same land.

The place itself would slowly become part of the experience.

Not a resort.

Not a retreat center separated from its surroundings.

But a **living rural base** where practical knowledge, nature, local culture and human connection come together.

**The aim is not scale for the sake of scale.**

The aim is to create something **lasting, repeatable and meaningful**—a place people can return to, contribute to and feel connected to over time.

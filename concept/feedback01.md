# UNPLUG TO RECONNECT — WEBSITE CONTENT FEEDBACK

## Overall direction

The website tone is strong, but in several places the copy becomes **too abstract or slogan-like** and loses some of the clarity of the original concept.

The priority should be:

- Keep the short, confident tone.
- Make sure a visitor understands the concept **without already knowing it**.
- Be explicit about **why the week exists**, **what participants take away**, and **what actually happens**.
- Use punchy lines as support, not as replacements for explanation.
- Preserve the three core outcomes from the concept: **Digital reset · Confidence · Real human connection**.

---

# 01 / THE POINT

## Current issue

The section is visually strong, but **“The Point” needs to explain the purpose of the experience more clearly**.

Current:

> We don’t offer answers. We make room to find your own direction.  
> Step away from digital noise. Do real things, spend time in nature, and share life face-to-face.

This is good, but it should connect more explicitly to the situation participants are in: young adulthood, important decisions, and the amount of input/comparison coming through screens.

## Suggested replacement

### THE POINT

## Step away from the digital noise for a week.

At 18–25, many of us are making some of our first big decisions about study, work, relationships, where we want to live and what kind of life we want.

At the same time, much of what we see, hear and compare ourselves with comes through a screen.

**We don’t offer answers. We create a different environment in which you might find your own direction, inspiration or perspective — through doing things, spending time in nature and talking face-to-face with other people.**

### WHAT YOU TAKE HOME

**01 / Digital reset**  
Move technology away from the centre of everyday life for a week and experience what changes when your phone is no longer the default place to turn.

**02 / Confidence**  
Hike, cook, camp, learn practical skills, take responsibility and discover more of what you can do for yourself.

**03 / Real human connection**  
Live, work, eat, disagree, laugh and spend time with a small group of people — face-to-face.

# 02 / A DAY AT CAMP

## Current issue

The section should make clear that this is a **general daily rhythm**, not a rigid timetable.

The current labels such as “Wake slowly” and “Use your hands or your head” are atmospheric, but the visitor should be able to scan the page and immediately understand:

**Breakfast → Morning workshop → Lunch → Free afternoon → Dinner → Evening together**

## Suggested intro

### A TYPICAL DAY AT CAMP

## A general rhythm, not a packed schedule.

Every day will be a little different, but most days follow the same simple structure. There is time to learn something, time to help run the camp, and plenty of time when nothing is scheduled.

### BREAKFAST

Start the day slowly. Eat breakfast together, drink coffee or tea, check the weather and get ready for the day.

### MORNING WORKSHOP

Each morning has a workshop slot. It might be a practical session where we learn by doing, or a discussion and debate session where we explore a topic together.

### LUNCH

Lunch is prepared and eaten together. Depending on the day, food may be prepared with the help of a local cook using simple regional ingredients and dishes.

### FREE AFTERNOON

Nothing needs to be scheduled every hour.

Read. Sleep. Walk. Play cards. Help in the garden. Talk. Sit somewhere. Have a ridiculous conversation or an important one.

**Learning how to spend unstructured time without automatically reaching for a phone is part of the experience.**

### DINNER

**Cook together.**

Everyone helps prepare dinner, clean up and look after the camp.

### EVENING

After dinner: campfire, games, music, conversations and stars.

# 03 / LEARN TOGETHER

## Current issue

This section needs to say clearly that it refers to the **morning workshop slots described above**.

“HANDS / PRACTICAL” and “VOICES / DISCUSSION” are good visual categories, but labels such as **“Relationships and identity”** are too broad without context.

Make it explicit that these are **possible workshop ideas**, not a fixed curriculum.

## Suggested replacement

### LEARN TOGETHER

## The morning workshop can take different forms.

Some mornings focus on **practical skills**: learning something by actually doing it.

Other mornings are **discussion and debate workshops**: a chance to explore a subject, hear different perspectives, ask questions and form your own view.

The exact mix is not fixed. It depends on the participants, mentors, local people and the place.

### POSSIBLE PRACTICAL WORKSHOPS

- Building and managing a campfire
- Planning and preparing for a mountain hike
- Navigation and reading a map
- Pitching and organising a camp
- Healthy food and nutrition
- Basic woodworking and craftsmanship
- Traditional rural skills and ways of working the land

These sessions could be led by mentors, craftspeople or local people with practical experience.

### POSSIBLE DISCUSSION & DEBATE TOPICS

- Relationships
- Gender, feminism and masculinity
- Social media and its influence
- Climate change
- Politics and society
- Food and consumption
- Capitalism and responsibility
- What success means
- The choices we make as we enter adulthood

These sessions are not about giving participants the “right” answer. A mentor or moderator introduces the subject, asks useful questions and creates space for different perspectives.

**Our role is to create the environment, not dictate the answer.**

# 04 / THE MOUNTAIN NIGHT

## Current issue

Current:

> Carry it in.  
> Sleep under stars.  
> Walk home.

This sounds good once someone already understands the idea, but it is **too abstract as the main explanation**.

Likewise:

> One night in the mountains, reached under your own power.

“Reached under your own power” is understandable English, but it sounds unnatural here and makes the experience less clear.

## Suggested replacement

### THE MOUNTAIN NIGHT

## Spend one night up in the mountains.

One of the defining experiences of the week is a **one-night mountain expedition starting directly from camp**.

We leave on foot carrying our backpacks, tents, sleeping bags, food and equipment. We hike into the mountains, set up camp, cook, spend the evening outside, sleep there for the night, watch the sunrise, pack everything again and walk back to base the next day.

**No excursion bus. No drive to a tourist attraction. We start and finish on foot from camp.**

This is meant to be a real challenge and include some intentional discomfort, but it is **not survival training**. Experienced leadership, proper planning, suitable equipment and safety remain fundamental.

### EQUIPMENT PROVIDED

- Proper hiking backpacks
- Reliable tents
- Sleeping bags suitable for temperatures around 0°C
- Necessary shared camping equipment

Optional short supporting line:

> Carry the pack. Set up camp. Spend the night in the mountains. Walk home the next day.

# 06 / QUESTIONS

The existing questions are strong and should stay:

### Can I use my phone, and can home reach me?

Phones are stored safely for most of the week. There are designated opportunities to contact family and friends, and the camp remains reachable in an emergency.

### Do I need hiking or camping experience?

No previous camping experience is expected. The week is built around learning by doing. Final fitness or participation requirements for the mountain expedition will be explained before applications open.

### Is this survival training, and what equipment is provided?

No. The mountain night is a planned outdoor challenge, not survival training. The camp provides proper hiking backpacks, reliable tents, sleeping bags suitable for temperatures around 0°C and the necessary shared equipment.

### When is it, and how much will it cost?

Dates and pricing for the first pilot are not final yet. Join the pilot list and we will share the details when applications open.

## Add these three questions

### Do I need to speak fluent English?

No. English is the common language of the camp, but it does not need to be your first language. The point is to use English naturally while cooking, hiking, talking and spending time together — not to speak perfectly.

### Where do we sleep?

The base camp uses simple outdoor accommodation in a rural setting, with tents and access to the basic facilities needed for the week. One night is spent camping in the mountains as part of the expedition. Exact accommodation details will be shared before booking.

### What will we eat?

Meals are part of the shared experience. We will cook and eat together, with an emphasis on simple, fresh food and local ingredients where possible. Dietary requirements and allergies should be collected during registration so the team can confirm what can be accommodated.

# CONTENT PRINCIPLE FOR THE WHOLE PAGE

**Clarity first. Character second.**

The short lines, labels and field-manual language are part of the brand and should stay, but a first-time visitor should never have to infer what an activity actually means.

A useful rule for the page:

> **Explain the experience clearly first. Then use the punchy line to make it memorable.**

For example:

**Spend one night up in the mountains.**  
We leave directly from camp on foot carrying our tents, food and sleeping equipment.

*Carry the pack. Set up camp. Watch the sunrise. Walk home.*

That balance preserves the personality of the site without sacrificing understanding.
